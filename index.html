Who is FewType for?

Three scenarios, three different kinds of people. You'll probably recognize yourself in at least one.

🎙️

Voice input — talk instead of typing

Press a global hotkey anywhere (Ctrl+Win or double-press Ctrl), speak, and it types for you — with the window focus restored before pasting.

  • Everyday chats & AI conversations — express fast, skip the typing labor
  • You care about how your output sounds — custom styles rewrite your words the way you want them said
  • One-key translation to screen — speak Chinese, output English (8 target languages)
📖

Ebook reading — listen instead of staring

A browser extension reads ebooks aloud in your browser (Google Play Books, Koodo Reader, EPUB readers). Not a robotic voice — sentence segmentation and synthesis are tuned for continuity.

  • You need a natural human voice and refuse machine-sounding TTS
  • Long focused reading tires your eyes — dual audio+visual channels keep you going
  • You read during fragments of time that can't be fully focused — commute, chores, eyes busy
🎧

Long-text TTS — for creators

Type or paste a long script, pick a voice, get a natural-voice audio file. A companion feature, but a lifesaver for people who produce spoken content.

  • Voice-over creators who don't want to record every long script by themselves
  • Feeding audio to digital humans / virtual presenters
  • Podcast drafts, video voice-over, audiobook prototyping, listening practice materials

Why not just use the built-in one?

Windows has voice typing. Wispr Flow has polish. Here's what FewType does differently.

vs. Windows built-in
Works in every app, not just system text boxes — browsers, terminals, chat windows, self-drawn menusFocus restoration + simulated paste means it lands where your cursor is, even in apps the system can't reach.
vs. paid dictation (Wispr Flow…)
Free & open source, bring your own key — no subscription, no vendor lock-inUse Groq, Volcengine, DeepSeek or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Your keys, your cost control.
vs. plain STT tools
Style rewriting + translation built in — dictation that also writes betterCustom styles (e.g. cinematic prose), one-key translation, and a same-language fast path that skips the LLM.
vs. TTS readers
The ebook reader ships with the app — one download covers dictation + readingSimplified/Traditional Chinese voices auto-match the book's script; long-text TTS covers the creator side.

What's inside

The essentials, in one glance.

🔑 Keys never leave your machine

API keys live in a protected local config. The installer ships with no keys — copying or re-packing the app triggers a security warning. Transcription text never touches logs.

🌱 Lightweight by design

Built on Tauri, not Electron — low memory footprint, quiet in the background, small installer. All the features, none of the bloat.

🌍 8 target languages

Simplified/Traditional Chinese, English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese, Korean — speak, translate, output.

⚡ Same-language fast path

Dictate Chinese → output Chinese without the LLM round-trip. Confirmation panel only appears when it's needed.

🧩 Extension included

Ebook reader extension for Chrome/Edge, adaptive to both the new Tauri app and the legacy TK line.

Open source — and honest about limits

FewType is an indie project. It doesn't pretend to be a mature team product, and that's part of the deal.

What you should know

  • You need your own API keys. FewType is BYOK — you register for the ASR / LLM / TTS services yourself. Recognition and translation quality depend on the service you pick.
  • Free, open source (MIT), no accounts, no telemetry. The code, the builds, the roadmap — all public.
  • Not code-signed. Windows SmartScreen may warn on first run — that's expected, click "More info → Run anyway".
  • Built by one developer, maintained through GitHub issues. Scope is defined by real use: voice input, ebook reading, long-text TTS — with clear boundaries around each.
⚠️ About the Windows first-run warning
FewType is a free, open-source personal project and doesn't yet pay for expensive enterprise code signing. On first install, Windows SmartScreen may show a warning. It's safe: click "More info" → "Run anyway". All your data and keys stay on this machine — the program never records raw dictation audio.
